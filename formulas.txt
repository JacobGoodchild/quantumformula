FORMULA 1 - Quantum geometry of the Lieb-lattice flat band: exact integrated and minimal
            quantum metric for staggered hopping (the quantity behind flat-band superfluidity)
Found: 2026-09-28.  Status: closed form found by PSLQ (60 digits, 6 values of delta) and then
checked by direct high-precision subtraction at random parameters (agreement to 1e-50) and by
an independent from-scratch numerical code (agreement ~1e-11, limited by finite differences).
Novelty: NOT FOUND in literature searched. The quantity is studied in current research, where
it seems to be computed numerically; the full texts could not be read from here (see NOVELTY).
==========================================================================================

THE QUESTION (plain English)
  In some crystals an electron band is perfectly "flat": the electrons in it have no kinetic
  energy at all, so by the usual reasoning they could not flow. Yet if they pair up
  (superconductivity / superfluidity of cold atoms) they CAN carry a current, and how easily
  they do so is set by the "quantum metric" of the band: a measure of how quickly the
  electron's quantum state changes shape as its momentum changes. Averaged over all momenta
  it is a pure number. The Lieb lattice (a square lattice with an extra atom in the middle of
  every edge) is the textbook flat-band crystal and the one used in cold-atom and photonic
  experiments. Making the bonds alternate strong/weak (strength 1+delta, 1-delta) isolates the
  flat band. What is the averaged quantum metric, exactly, as a function of delta?

MODEL / SET-UP
  Lieb lattice, lattice constant 1: corner site A at (0,0), edge sites B at (s,0), C at (0,s).
  Hopping A-B and A-C inside the unit cell: 1+delta_x (resp. 1+delta_y); between cells:
  1-delta_x (resp. 1-delta_y).  Bloch Hamiltonian (orbital positions included in the phases)
     H(k) = [[0, f, g], [f*, 0, 0], [g*, 0, 0]],
     f = (1+dx) e^{i kx s} + (1-dx) e^{-i kx (1-s)},  g = same with ky, dy.
  The band E = 0 is exactly flat, with Bloch vector psi = (0, g, -f)/sqrt(|f|^2+|g|^2); for
  dx, dy != 0 it is separated from the other bands by a gap.
  Quantum metric  g_ij(k) = Re<d_i psi|(1-|psi><psi|)|d_j psi>.
  Integrated metric  M = (1/(2pi)^2) Int_BZ [g_xx + g_yy] d^2k   (dimensionless, a = 1).
  K(k), E(k) = complete elliptic integrals of the first/second kind with MODULUS k
  (mpmath: ellipk(k^2), ellipe(k^2)).

THE FORMULA
  (a) Isotropic staggering dx = dy = delta, orbitals at the bond midpoints (s = 1/2):
        M = ( 2 K(k) - E(k) ) / (4 pi),        k = (1 - delta^2)/(1 + delta^2).
      Examples:  delta = 1/2 (k = 3/5):  M = 0.16579363089945170052923373923434968074939742050657
                 delta = 3/10:           M = 0.23065200969101156099961480898799757884...
      delta -> 1 (isolated trimers): M -> 1/8.   delta -> 0: M ~ (1/(2pi)) ln(2/delta) - 1/(4pi),
      the logarithmic divergence of the band touching (k' = 2 delta/(1+delta^2)).

  (b) Anisotropic staggering, s = 1/2.  With k_x = (1-dx^2)/(1+dx^2), k_y = (1-dy^2)/(1+dy^2):
        M = (2 + k_x + k_y) / ( 8 sqrt((1+k_x)(1+k_y)) ) * ( 2 K(k) - E(k) ) / pi,
        with k^2 = k_x k_y   (i.e. elliptic PARAMETER m = k_x k_y).
      Equivalently the prefactor is (2+dx^2+dy^2)/(8 sqrt((1+dx^2)(1+dy^2))).
      Example dx = 3/7, dy = 5/11: M = 0.17980378519008855485546405197534216451695784433779

  (d) The separate directional components (anisotropic staggering, s = 1/2) - these set the
      superfluid-weight TENSOR D_xx, D_yy of a flat-band superconductor on this lattice.
      Added later on 2026-09-28 (PSLQ at 60 digits on 28 (k_x,k_y) pairs plus 22 pairs with k_x = 1/2 with basis {sqrt((1+k_x)(1+k_y)) K,
      same x E}; the coefficients fitted by exact rational interpolation):
        Int g_xx = sqrt((1+k_y)/(1+k_x)) / (16 pi) * [ 4 K(k) - (1-k_x)(2+k_x+k_y)/(1-k_x k_y) * E(k) ],
        k^2 = k_x k_y;  Int g_yy = same with x <-> y.
      Checks: vs 50-digit quadrature at (dx,dy) = (0.37,0.81), (0.1,0.9), (3/7,5/11), (0.05,0.6): all
      differences < 2e-51; g_xx + g_yy reproduces (b) to < 2e-51; isotropic case gives (2K-E)/(8 pi) = half
      of (a). Independent finite differences (160^2 mesh) at (0.37,0.81): g_xx 0.08698816772405 (formula
      0.08698816772687), g_yy 0.05642969590245 (formula 0.05642969590400).
      Code: code/lieb_metric/gxx_fit3.py, gxx_formula.py.
  (e) A SINGLE distorted bond (added later on 2026-09-28): intra-cell x-bond hopping a, the other three
      hoppings 1 (s = 1/2). One modified bond already isolates the flat band (for a != 1). Then
        M(a) = (a^2+3) / ( 2 pi (a+1) sqrt(a^2-2a+5) ) * [ K(k) - (a+1)^2/(a^2+2a+5) * E(k) ],
        k^2 = 16 a / ( (a+1)^2 (a^2-2a+5) ).
      a = 1 gives k = 1 and the (2K-E)/(4pi) form (gapless limit). Values: a = 3: 0.1066317423090678225451329271 (k^2 = 3/8);
      a = 1.37: 0.3582868489357676071833259; a = 0.25: 0.2809556618821731184701665.
      Found by PSLQ (60 digits, basis {K/pre, E/pre}) at 9 values of a and exact rational interpolation;
      checked at a = 1.37, e (2.718281828), 0.6, 0.25 against 50-digit quadrature (diffs < 2e-51) and by
      finite differences at a = 1.37 (0.35828684885445 vs 0.35828684893577; near-gapless, slower mesh
      convergence). The fully general four-hopping case is K and E only as well (NOTES.md), not yet fitted.
      Code: code/lieb_metric/lieb4.py, lieb4_family.py, lieb4_family_formula.py.
  (f) TWO distorted bonds (generalises (a) and (e); added later on 2026-09-28): intra-cell hoppings a (x-bond)
      and b (y-bond), both inter-cell hoppings 1, s = 1/2. With
        S1 = (a-1)^2+(b-1)^2,  S2 = (a+1)^2+(b+1)^2,  R1 = (a+1)^2+(b-1)^2,  R2 = (a-1)^2+(b+1)^2:
        M(a,b) = (a^2+b^2+2) / (2 pi sqrt(R1 R2)) * [ K(k) - ((a^2-1)^2 + (b^2-1)^2)/(S1 S2) * E(k) ],
        k^2 = 16 a b / (R1 R2).
      Symmetric in a <-> b; b = 1 gives (e); a = b gives (2K-E)/(4pi) with k = 2a/(1+a^2), i.e. (a) with
      a = (1+delta)/(1-delta). (Note: this slice differs from (b), where the x- and y-hopping SUMS are equal.)
      Values: (a,b) = (1.7,0.45): 0.2277337726004986157296796;  (pi,e) ~ (3.14159,2.71828): 0.1646130393872643...;
              (5,0.2): 0.02303519056398397395420779.
      Found by PSLQ at 90 rational (a,b) pairs (basis {K/pre, E/pre}); K-coefficient (a^2+b^2+2)/(4a) holds at
      all 90; the E-coefficient is the unique rational function of total degree <= 4 through all 90 points
      (null space of dimension 1 for 30 unknowns). Checks: vs 50-digit quadrature at (1.7,0.45), (3.14159,
      2.71828), (0.3,0.8), (5,0.2): diffs < 7e-52; independent finite differences at (1.7,0.45):
      0.22773377257857 (formula 0.22773377260050).
      Code: code/lieb_metric/lieb4_two.py, lieb4_two_fit.py, lieb_two_formula.py.
  (c) Minimal quantum metric (minimum over all orbital positions; isotropic delta).
      M depends on where the orbitals are placed; it is exactly quadratic in the B-C relative
      position, and by the x<->y symmetry the minimum lies on the family s above. Result:
        M_min = [ (2K - E) E - k'^2 K^2 ] / ( 4 pi E ),     k' = sqrt(1-k^2) = 2 delta/(1+delta^2),
        attained at  s* = 1/2 - k' K(k) / (2 E(k)).
      Examples: delta = 1/2: M_min = 0.0557110773516971020044417811308, s* = 0.00616337240269203...
                delta = 1/4: M_min = 0.1847533407197373898290808843634, s* = 0.06381945082763012...
      delta -> 1: M_min -> 0 and s* -> 0 (orbitals collapse onto the trimer centre), as it must.

DERIVATION (sketch)
  psi is a CP^1 state with ratio z = -f/g, so g_xx = |f'|^2 |g|^2/(|f|^2+|g|^2)^2 etc.
  With a = |f|^2 = 2(1+d^2) + 2(1-d^2) cos kx one finds |f'|^2 = (1+d^2) - a/4 (for s = 1/2),
  hence Tr g = (1+d^2)/S - ab/(2 S^2), S = a + b = 4(1+d^2)[1 + (k/2)(cos kx + cos ky)].
  <1/S> is the square-lattice Green function = K(k)/(2 pi (1+d^2)); <ab/S^2> reduces to it and
  its k-derivative and equals E(k)/(2 pi). This gives (a). For a general position s the extra
  phase adds 4 delta t b/S^2 + t^2 ab/S^2 to g_xx (t = s - 1/2; uses Im(f* f') = 2 delta), and
  similarly for g_yy, so M(t) = M(0) + 4 delta t <1/S> + 2 t^2 <ab/S^2>; minimising gives (c).
  (b) was identified by PSLQ with basis {1, K/pi, E/pi, (sqrt-weighted variants)} and then the
  rational prefactor fitted from 7 parameter pairs; it reduces to (a) when dx = dy.

VERIFICATION
  1. PSLQ at 60 digits, delta = 1/2, 1/3, 1/5, 2/3, 3/4, 1/7: in every case the relation
     4 M - 2 K/pi + E/pi = 0 is returned (no other relation with coefficients < 1e8).
  2. Formula (b) minus direct 50-digit quadrature (inner integral done exactly, outer by
     tanh-sinh): (3/7,5/11) diff -3e-52; (0.1,0.9) diff 0; (0.37,0.81) diff 0 at 50 digits.
  3. Independent code (check_numeric2.py): numerical diagonalisation of H(k), flat-band
     projector P, metric = (1/2) Tr(dP dP) by central finite differences, 160x160 k-mesh:
        (3/7,5/11): 0.17980378518293   vs formula 0.17980378519009
        (0.37,0.81): 0.14341786362649  vs formula 0.14341786363087
        delta = 1/2, s = 1/2: 0.16579363089380 vs 0.16579363089945
  4. Minimal metric: numerical minimisation over s with the independent code:
        delta = 1/2: min 0.0557110773484 at s = 0.0061638   (formula 0.0557110773517, 0.0061634)
        delta = 1/4: min 0.1847533407024 at s = 0.0638195   (formula 0.1847533407197, 0.0638195)

FORMULA 2 - Exact integrated quantum metric (gauge-invariant Wannier spread) of the
            Qi-Wu-Zhang Chern insulator, one formula for every mass m (both phases)
Found: 2026-09-28.  Status: inner BZ integral done exactly (symbolic residues), outer by 60-digit
quadrature; closed form found by PSLQ at 8 values of m > 2 and coefficient functions fitted;
the resulting single formula then checked against 50-digit quadrature at 10 further values of m
covering both phases and negative m (agreement ~1e-51), against a slow direct 2D quadrature,
and against an independent finite-difference code (~1e-10).
Novelty: NOT FOUND in literature searched (quantity is widely computed numerically).
==========================================================================================

THE QUESTION (plain English)
  The Qi-Wu-Zhang model is the simplest lattice model of a "Chern insulator": an insulator whose
  electrons carry a quantised Hall current along the edges (for 0 < |m| < 2) or not (|m| > 2).
  The topology is captured by an integer (the Chern number). The quantum metric measures,
  in addition, how "spread out" the electrons' Wannier orbitals must be: averaged over the
  Brillouin zone it equals the minimal possible spread of localised electron orbitals
  (Marzari-Vanderbilt invariant Omega_I). It is not quantised, and is always at least
  |Chern|/(2 pi). What is it, exactly, as a function of m?

MODEL / SET-UP
  H(k) = sin kx sigma_x + sin ky sigma_y + (m + cos kx + cos ky) sigma_z, lower band filled.
  Quantum metric g_ij of the lower band; for a two-band model
     Tr g = sum_i [ |d_i d|^2 |d|^2 - (d . d_i d)^2 ] / (4 |d|^4).
  M(m) = (1/(2pi)^2) Int_BZ (g_xx + g_yy) d^2k  ( = Omega_I / (2pi) per unit cell, a = 1 ).
  K(k), Pi(n,k): complete elliptic integrals of the 1st and 3rd kind, with PARAMETER k^2
  (mpmath: ellipk(k2), ellippi(n, k2)); Pi(n,k) = Int_0^{pi/2} dt/((1-n sin^2 t) sqrt(1-k^2 sin^2 t)).

THE FORMULA
  For every real m != 0, 1, +-2 (m = -1 is fine):

    M(m) = 1/8 - (m^2 - 4m + 2)/(4 m^2 (m-1)) * K(k)/pi - (m-2)^2/(4 m (m-1)) * Pi(n,k)/pi,

    with  k^2 = 8 (m^2 - 2)/m^4,   n = 4 (m-1)/m^2.

  (M is even in m; for 0 < |m| < sqrt2, k^2 < 0 and the formula is used as it stands - the real
   analytic continuation, which mpmath evaluates directly. The value at m = 1 is the limit.)

  Special values:
    m = 1:  M = [ 3 + (7 K - 9 E)/pi ] / 24  with k^2 = 8/9 (E = 2nd kind)
             = 0.22681551281725283816876252594820706177
    m = 3:  M = 1/8 + (K(k) - 3 Pi(8/9,k))/(72 pi), k^2 = 56/81, = 0.03874466795035547949099350085
    m = 4:  M = 1/8 - (K + 8 Pi(3/4,k))/(96 pi), k^2 = 7/16, = 0.01854679578624327731478554388
    m = 1/2: 0.286294083377026488160991...   m = 3/2: 0.19415426221702556424...
  ALSO MINIMAL: both orbitals sit on the same site, which is a C4 fixed point; M is quadratic in the
  (fictitious) orbital offset and C4-invariant, so its gradient vanishes there: this M is also the
  MINIMAL quantum metric (the one that controls flat-band-type superfluid weight bounds).
  Limits: m -> infinity: M -> 0.  At the gap closing m -> 2, k' = |m^2-4|/m^2 -> 0 and
  M ~ -(1/(8 pi)) ln|m - 2| (logarithmic divergence from the Dirac cone).  m -> 0: diverges
  (two Dirac cones). Throughout the topological phase M > 1/(2 pi) = 0.159, as required (scan of 0<m<2 in steps
  of 0.005: minimum M = 0.188743 at m ~ 1.715).

DERIVATION (sketch)
  Tr g is a rational function of cos kx, cos ky, and |d|^2 = A + B cos ky with
  A = 2 + m^2 + 2m cos kx, B = 2(m + cos kx) is LINEAR in cos ky. So the ky-average is done
  exactly (partial fractions in u = A + B cos ky; <1/u> = D^{-1/2}, <1/u^2> = A D^{-3/2}),
  with D = A^2 - B^2 = (m^4 + 4) + 4 m^3 c + 4 (m^2 - 1) c^2, c = cos kx.
  D has roots c = -(m^2-2m+2)/(2(m-1)) and -(m^2+2m+2)/(2(m+1)) (discriminant 16(m^2-2)^2),
  so the kx-average is a complete elliptic integral over the four branch points {1,-1,r1,r2}:
  k^2 = cross-ratio = 8(m^2-2)/m^4, and the rational factors produce a third-kind integral with
  characteristic n = 4(m-1)/m^2. Coefficients were identified by PSLQ (basis {1,K/pi,E/pi,Pi/pi};
  E drops out) at m = 9/4, 5/2, 3, 7/2, 4, 5, 6, 8 and fitted to rational functions:
  coefficient of K/pi times 4m^2(m-1) gave 1, -2, -7, -14, -34 ... = -(m^2-4m+2), etc.

VERIFICATION
  1. PSLQ, 60 digits, 8 values of m > 2 and 4 values in 1 < m < 2: every relation has the stated
     coefficients (e.g. m = 5/4 gives K-coefficient 137/351 and Pi-coefficient -125/1404, both
     exactly as the formula predicts).
  2. Unified formula minus 50-digit quadrature: m = 0.5: 0; 0.333: 0; 0.9: 3e-51; 1.1: -2e-51;
     1.7: 7e-52; 2.5: -1e-51; 7: 5e-53; 0.05: 0; -0.5: 1e-51; -3: -3e-52.
  3. m = 1: limit of the formula from both sides (difference 2e-36 at offset 1e-35) equals
     [3 + (7K-9E)/pi]/24 (PSLQ at 40 digits); a slow direct 2D quadrature gives 0.226815512817253.
  4. Independent code (check_fd.py): numerical diagonalisation, projector finite differences,
     200x200 mesh:  m=1: 0.2268155128033 (formula 0.2268155128173);  m=0.5: 0.2862940833516
     (0.2862940833770);  m=3: 0.0387446679478 (0.0387446679504);  m=1.7: 0.1887768903908
     (0.1887768904159).

FORMULA 3 - Exact integrated quantum metric (Wannier spread) of gapped graphene / hBN-type
            honeycomb insulator, for any sublattice mass Delta; exactly 1/48 at Delta = 3t
Found: 2026-09-28.  Status: DERIVED analytically (divergence-theorem reduction to the
triangular-lattice Green function + standard elliptic reduction), then verified against
60-digit spectrally-accurate BZ quadrature (agreement 1e-54 to 1e-62) and an independent
finite-difference code (~1e-11).  Novelty: NOT FOUND in literature searched.
==========================================================================================

THE QUESTION (plain English)
  Graphene is a honeycomb sheet of carbon atoms; its electrons behave like massless particles.
  If the two kinds of site in the honeycomb are made different (as in boron nitride, or graphene
  on a boron-nitride substrate) by an energy +Delta / -Delta, a band gap opens and the sheet
  becomes an insulator. How spread out, at minimum, are the electrons' localised (Wannier)
  orbitals in this insulator? That minimal spread is the Brillouin-zone-averaged quantum
  metric, a directly measurable quantity (it equals an optical sum rule, Souza-Wilkens-Martin).

MODEL / SET-UP
  Honeycomb tight binding, hopping t = 1, on-site energy +Delta on A and -Delta on B:
     H(k) = [[Delta, f], [f*, -Delta]],  f(k) = sum_{j=1..3} exp(i k.delta_j),
  delta_j = the three nearest-neighbour vectors (physical orbital positions used; Bravais
  lattice constant a = 1, so the C-C distance is 1/sqrt3). Lower band filled.
  M(Delta) = (1/A_BZ) Int_BZ (g_xx + g_yy) d^2k    (units of a^2; multiply by 3 for units
  of the C-C distance squared).  K(k) complete elliptic integral, PARAMETER k^2 (mpmath ellipk).

THE FORMULA
     M(Delta) = 1/48 + (9 - Delta^2) K(k) / ( 24 pi (1+Delta^2)^{3/4} (9+Delta^2)^{1/4} ),

     k^2 = 1/2 + (3 - 6 Delta^2 - Delta^4) / ( 2 (1+Delta^2) sqrt((1+Delta^2)(9+Delta^2)) ).

  Equivalently M = 1/48 + (9 - Delta^2)/48 * G_tri(3 + Delta^2), where
  G_tri(w) = <1/(w + 2cos k1 + 2cos k2 + 2cos(k1-k2))> is the triangular-lattice Green function.
  Values:
    Delta = 1:   k^2 = (sqrt5 - 1)/(2 sqrt5) (golden ratio appears);
                 M = 0.08115743880582535287728367820536975818157...
    Delta = 1/2: M = 0.1352237617547092930141117606332085744003...
    Delta = 2:   M = 0.03741227989852118050121467521925664006501...
    Delta = 3:   M = 1/48 EXACTLY (= 1/16 in units of the C-C distance squared).
  ALSO MINIMAL: the physical B position tau* = delta_1 is the fixed point of the affine symmetry
  tau -> R tau + a_1 (R = C3 rotation about A), and M is quadratic in tau, so grad M(tau*) = 0: the value
  above is also the MINIMAL quantum metric over orbital positions. (Same argument for diamond with its
  tetrahedral symmetry, Formula 4.)
  Numerical confirmation (check_minimal_honey.py, Delta = 1): moving the B orbital by 0.05 in six
  different directions raises M by 3.98785e-4 in every case.
  Limits (checked numerically): Delta -> infinity: M ~ 1/(4 Delta^2) (Delta^2 M = 0.2499991 at
  Delta = 1000).  Delta -> 0: k -> 1 and M diverges logarithmically (the two Dirac cones),
  M ~ (sqrt3/(8 pi)) ln(1/Delta) + const (slope 0.0689161119 = sqrt3/(8 pi) at Delta ~ 1e-6).

DERIVATION (sketch)
  For d = (Re f, Im f, Delta):  Tr g = (1/4) [ |grad f|^2/(|f|^2+D^2) - |grad |f|^2|^2 /(4(|f|^2+D^2)^2) ].
  Two lattice facts make this collapse:
   (i)  |grad f|^2 = sum_{j,l} delta_j.delta_l e^{ik(delta_j-delta_l)} = 1 - eps/6, where
        eps = |f|^2 - 3 = 2 sum_j cos(k.a_j) is the triangular-lattice dispersion (|delta|^2 = 1/3,
        delta_j.delta_l = -1/6 for j != l);
   (ii) Laplacian(eps) = -eps (all |a_j| = 1), so by the divergence theorem on the BZ torus
        <|grad eps|^2/(eps+w)^2> = <grad eps . grad(-1/(eps+w))> = <(1/(eps+w)) Lap eps>
        = -<eps/(eps+w)> ... = w G - 1  (G = <1/(eps+w)>, w = 3 + D^2).
  Hence M = (1/4)[(1 + w/6) G - 1/6] - (1/16)(w G - 1) = 1/48 + (12 - w) G/48.
  G_tri(w): the k2-average is exact, 1/sqrt((w+2c)^2 - 8(1+c)), c = cos k1; the quadratic has the
  complex-conjugate roots c = (-1 - D^2 +- 2iD)/2, and the standard reduction (Byrd-Friedman
  259.00) gives G = K(k)/(pi sqrt(A B)), A^2 = (1+D^2)(9+D^2)/4, B = (1+D^2)/2,
  k^2 = (4 - (A-B)^2)/(4AB), which simplifies to the expression above.
  The special point Delta = 3 is where the Green-function term is multiplied by zero.

FORMULA 4 - Quantum metric of gapped "simplex" bipartite lattices: a general exact reduction,
            a universal "magic mass" where the answer is rational, and the DIAMOND lattice
            in closed form (elliptic K squared)
Found: 2026-09-28.  Status: DERIVED analytically (generalising Formula 3); the diamond closed form
uses Joyce's FCC Green function, whose continuation below the band was checked (25 digits).
Verified against a direct 30-digit 3D Brillouin-zone quadrature (exponentially convergent: N=24
diff 2e-11, N=36 diff 2e-16 at Delta=1; 33 digits at the magic mass) and an independent
vectorised finite-difference code (~1e-12).  Novelty: NOT FOUND in literature searched.
==========================================================================================

THE QUESTION (plain English)
  Formula 3 found that the minimal spread of electron orbitals in gapped graphene becomes a
  simple fraction at one special value of the gap. Is that an accident of graphene? No: the same
  happens for every crystal of the same "shape" (each atom bonded to neighbours that sit at the
  corners of a regular triangle, tetrahedron, ...), in any dimension. The 3D example is the
  diamond lattice (diamond, silicon; with unequal atoms: zinc-blende semiconductors such as
  GaAs, in the simplest tight-binding picture).

MODEL / SET-UP
  Bipartite lattice, each A site bonded to z B sites along vectors delta_1..delta_z that form a
  regular simplex: |delta_j| = b, sum delta_j = 0 (so delta_j.delta_l = -b^2/(z-1), j != l), and
  the differences delta_j - delta_l all have length L, L^2 = 2 b^2 z/(z-1).
  (Honeycomb: z = 3; diamond: z = 4; the d-dimensional "hyperdiamond": z = d+1.)
  H(k) = [[Delta, f],[f*, -Delta]], f = sum_j e^{i k.delta_j} (hopping 1, physical positions).
  M = BZ average of Tr g of the lower band (sum over all d Cartesian directions).
  eps(k) = |f|^2 - z = sum_{j != l} e^{ik(delta_j - delta_l)} is the dispersion of the
  (second-neighbour) Bravais lattice; G(w) = <1/(eps + w)>, w = z + Delta^2.

THE FORMULA
  (a) General reduction:
        M = b^2 (z-2) / (8 (z-1))  +  b^2 [ z/4 - w (z-2)/(8(z-1)) ] G(z + Delta^2).
  (b) Magic mass: the Green-function term vanishes at
        Delta*^2 = z^2/(z-2),   where   M = b^2 (z-2)/(8(z-1))   exactly.
      Honeycomb: Delta* = 3, M = b^2/16 (= 1/48 in units of the Bravais constant; Formula 3).
      Diamond:   Delta* = 2 sqrt2, M = b^2/12 (= 1/64 in units of the cubic cell side a).
  (c) Diamond lattice, cubic cell side a = 1 (b^2 = 3/16), any Delta > 0:
        M(Delta) = 1/64 + (8 - Delta^2) (2 sqrt(4+Delta^2) - sqrt(16+Delta^2)) K(k)^2 / (16 pi^2 Delta^3),
        k^2 = 1/2 - [ (8+Delta^2) sqrt(16+Delta^2) - 16 sqrt(4+Delta^2) ] / (2 Delta^3)
      (k^2 is negative; K(k) with negative parameter is real, K(-m) = K(m/(1+m))/sqrt(1+m)).
      Values: Delta = 1:   M = 0.05096119856620922135431930085948489759852
              Delta = 2:   M = 0.02476385005653150256591406204766518706627
              Delta = 1/2: M = 0.08586494992921068599326746276626410764527
              Delta = 2 sqrt2: M = 1/64 exactly.
      Limits (numerical): Delta -> infinity: M ~ 3/(16 Delta^2); Delta -> 0: M ~ (3/(32 pi^2)) ln^2(1/Delta)
      (squared logarithm = the nodal LINES of the massless diamond model; cf. single log for the
      Dirac POINTS of graphene).

DERIVATION (sketch)
  As in Formula 3: Tr g = (1/4)[|grad f|^2/(|f|^2+D^2) - |grad|f|^2|^2/(4(|f|^2+D^2)^2)].
  For a simplex star, |grad f|^2 = z b^2 + c eps with c = -b^2/(z-1), and Laplacian(eps) = -L^2 eps,
  so the divergence theorem gives <|grad eps|^2/(eps+w)^2> = L^2 (w G - 1). Collecting terms gives (a);
  the constant is (b^2 + c)/8 and the G coefficient z b^2/4 - w(c/4 + L^2/16), which vanishes at
  w = 2z(z-1)/(z-2), i.e. (b). For diamond eps = 4(c1c2 + c2c3 + c3c1), c_i = cos(k_i/2), so
  G(w) = -(1/4) g_FCC(-w/4) with Joyce's closed form of the FCC Green function (as implemented in the predecessor project),
  g_FCC(t) = 3^{3/2}(3+x)^{-3/2}(2 - sqrt(1-x)) (2K(k)/pi)^2 / t, x = 3/t, analytically continued
  below the band (t < -1); substituting t = -(4+D^2)/4 simplifies to (c).

VERIFICATION

     t = -1.5: diff 6e-26; t = -3: diff -6e-27.
  2. Explicit (c) vs the Joyce-based form: diffs <= 2e-39 at Delta = 1, 2, 0.5, 0.1, 5 (40 digits).

     onto the formula); N = 50: diff 2.4e-22.  Delta = 2 sqrt2: N = 36 gives 0.015625000000000000000000000000001 (1/64, diff 7e-34).
  4. Independent vectorised code (check_fd_diamond.py; unit-vector finite differences, FCC primitive
     reciprocal mesh 56^3):  D = 1: 0.050961198563718 (formula 0.050961198566209); D = 2: 0.024763850055714
     (0.024763850056532); D = 0.5: 0.085864949920787 (0.085864949929211); D = sqrt8: 0.015624999999547.
  5. (a) reproduces Formula 3 exactly for z = 3, b^2 = 1/3.

FORMULA 5 - Flat bands of the 3D Lieb (perovskite / edge-centred cubic) lattice: exact quantum
            metric via the simple-cubic Green function; at zero staggering it is exactly
            Watson's integral / 4
Found: 2026-09-28.  Status: DERIVED analytically (divergence-theorem reduction as in Formulas 3-4),
checked against an independent 4x4-Hamiltonian finite-difference code (1e-11 at delta = 0.5;
6e-8 at delta = 0.2 on a coarse mesh; delta = 0 extrapolates to Watson/4 within ~1e-4) and by the
delta -> 0 limit (converges linearly onto W/4, slope -sqrt3/pi).  Novelty: NOT FOUND.
==========================================================================================

THE QUESTION (plain English)
  The 3D cousin of the Lieb lattice puts an atom at every corner of a cubic grid and one in the
  middle of every edge (the oxygen-copper network of perovskites is of this type). It has TWO
  perfectly flat bands. With alternating strong/weak bonds (1+delta, 1-delta) they are isolated
  from the rest. How large is their quantum metric (the quantity that sets flat-band
  superfluid stiffness and Wannier spreads)? And what happens when delta -> 0 and the flat bands
  touch the other bands - in 2D the answer blows up (Formula 1), in 3D?

MODEL / SET-UP
  Corner site A at (0,0,0), edge sites at (1/2,0,0), (0,1/2,0), (0,0,1/2); hopping 1+delta inside
  the cell, 1-delta between cells.  H(k) = [[0, f1, f2, f3], [f1*,0,0,0], [f2*,0,0,0], [f3*,0,0,0]],
  f_i = (1+delta) e^{i k_i/2} + (1-delta) e^{-i k_i/2}.  E = 0 is a doubly degenerate flat band.
  M(delta) = BZ average of Tr g of the 2-dim flat-band projector (non-Abelian metric, summed over
  x, y, z; lattice constant 1).
  G(w) = <1/(w - cos k1 - cos k2 - cos k3)> = simple-cubic lattice Green function (w >= 3);
  Watson's integral W = 3 G(3) = (sqrt6/(32 pi^3)) Gamma(1/24)Gamma(5/24)Gamma(7/24)Gamma(11/24)
                       = 1.516386059151978018156012...

THE FORMULA
     M(delta) = (w/4) G(w) + ((w^2 - 9)/12) G'(w),      w = 3 (1 + delta^2)/(1 - delta^2).

  (G'(w) = dG/dw < 0.)  Values (30 digits, stable between 40 and 60 digits to 6e-42):
     delta = 0.5: M = 0.2015225399162324063211328...
     delta = 0.2: M = 0.284738118268315223493763710923
     delta = 0.1: M = 0.3279130727215118726645707...
  delta -> 0 (flat bands touching the dispersive bands at the R point):
     M(0) = (3/4) G(3) = W/4 = (sqrt6/(128 pi^3)) Gamma(1/24)Gamma(5/24)Gamma(7/24)Gamma(11/24)
          = 0.37909651478799450453900303992,
  FINITE, unlike the 2D Lieb lattice (log divergence, Formula 1). Numerically
     M(delta) = W/4 - (sqrt3/pi) delta + O(delta^2)
  ((W/4 - M)/delta = 0.547396, 0.550936, 0.551290, 0.551325 at delta = 1e-2..1e-5 -> sqrt3/pi = 0.551329).

DERIVATION (sketch)
  The flat subspace is the orthogonal complement (within the three edge orbitals) of n = f/|f|, so
  its metric equals that of the single vector n: Tr g = sum_i |f_i'|^2 (S - a_i)/S^2 with
  a_i = |f_i|^2 = 2p + 2q cos k_i, |f_i'|^2 = p - a_i/4, S = sum a_i, p = 1+delta^2, q = 1-delta^2.
  So Tr g = 2p/S - 1/4 + sum a_i^2/(4 S^2), and sum a_i^2 contains sum cos^2 k_i = 3 - |grad u|^2,
  u = sum cos k_i. Since Laplacian(u) = -u, the divergence theorem turns <|grad u|^2/S^2> into
  -<u/S>/(2q). Everything reduces to <1/(w+u)> and <1/(w+u)^2>; the constants cancel exactly and
  p^2 - q^2 = 4 delta^2 = q^2 (w^2-9)/9, giving the formula. As w -> 3, G'(w) ~ -c/sqrt(w-3), so the
  second term vanishes like sqrt(w-3) ~ delta, leaving W/4.
  GENERAL DIMENSION: the same steps for the d-dimensional Lieb lattice (d-1 flat bands) give
     M_d = (w/4) G_d(w) + ((w^2 - d^2)/(4d)) G_d'(w),   w = d(1+delta^2)/(1-delta^2),
  with G_d the hypercubic Green function. Consistency checks done by hand: d = 2, G_2 = (k/pi)K(k),
  k = 2/w, reproduces Formula 1 exactly ((2K-E)/(4 pi)); d = 1 gives 0 (no flat band), as it must.
  (G itself has a known closed form in complete elliptic integrals due to Joyce; here G is computed
   as a 1D integral of the square-lattice Green function (2/(pi z)) K(4/z^2).)

VERIFICATION

       delta = 0.2: N=20: 0.28473805310941                             (formula 0.28473811826832)
       delta = 0:   N=16: 0.36156695, N=24: 0.36746458, N=32: 0.37038673, N=40: 0.37213391 (error ~ 0.279/N);

FORMULA 6 - Berry-curvature fluctuations of the Qi-Wu-Zhang Chern insulator: <Omega^2> in closed
            form (complete elliptic K and E only), for every mass m
Found: 2026-09-28.  Status: inner BZ integral exact (symbolic), PSLQ at 70 digits for m = 3..24,
coefficient functions obtained by exact rational interpolation (all 22 points reproduced), then
the formula tested at 9 unseen values in both phases (agreement ~1e-51), against slow direct 2D

Novelty: NOT FOUND in literature searched.

THE QUESTION (plain English)
  In a Chern insulator the "Berry curvature" Omega(k) acts like a magnetic field living in
  momentum space; its total is fixed by topology (the Chern number), but it can be spread evenly
  or bunched up. How uneven it is - its mean square - is the standard diagnostic of whether a band
  can host fractional quantum Hall-like states (fractional Chern insulators need it nearly uniform).
  For the simplest lattice Chern insulator (Qi-Wu-Zhang), what is <Omega^2> exactly?

MODEL / SET-UP
  H(k) = sin kx sigma_x + sin ky sigma_y + (m + cos kx + cos ky) sigma_z, lower band; lattice const 1.
  Omega(k) = d.(d_x d x d_y d)/(2|d|^3) (so that Int Omega d^2k = 2 pi C).
  <Omega^2> = (1/(2pi)^2) Int_BZ Omega^2 d^2k.  For any two-band model det g = Omega^2/4, so this is
  also 4 <det g>.  K, E: complete elliptic integrals with PARAMETER k^2.

THE FORMULA
     <Omega^2> = (1/pi) [ (m^4 - 2m^2 - 4) / (32 m^2 (m^2-2)) * K(k)
                          - (m^6 - 6m^4 - 4m^2 + 16) / (32 (m^2-4)^2 (m^2-2)) * E(k) ],
     k^2 = 8(m^2 - 2)/m^4    (same modulus as Formula 2; valid for all real m != 0, +-2; at m^2 = 2
                             the two terms combine to a finite limit).
  Values:
     m = 1:  <Omega^2> = (5 K + 7 E)/(96 pi), k^2 = 8/9, = 0.06777120989668022179267735654237369684316
     m = 3:  (59/2016) K/pi - (223/5600) E/pi, k^2 = 56/81, = 0.00341262638757655770574008592917
     m = 1/2: 0.1170393103169380607691435...    m = 3/2: 0.1104084733895183185827042...
  In the topological phase <Omega> = 1/(2 pi), so the variance is <Omega^2> - 1/(4 pi^2)
  (e.g. m = 1: 0.0677712 - 0.0253303 = 0.0424409).

DERIVATION (sketch)
  Omega^2 = (d.(d_x d x d_y d))^2/(4|d|^6) is rational in cos kx, cos ky, and |d|^2 = A + B cos ky is
  linear in cos ky (A = 2 + m^2 + 2m cos kx, B = 2(m + cos kx)); so the ky-average is exact
  (<u^-n> from derivatives of (A^2-B^2)^{-1/2}). The remaining kx-integral has the same four branch
  points as in Formula 2, hence modulus k^2 = 8(m^2-2)/m^4. Unlike the metric, no third-kind integral
  and no constant appear (PSLQ with basis {1, K, E, Pi} returns zero coefficients for 1 and Pi).
  Coefficients a(m), b(m) found at m = 3..24 and interpolated as exact rational functions.

FORMULA 8 - Berry-curvature fluctuations of the gapped DIAMOND lattice (3D): exact expression in
            the FCC lattice Green function and its first two derivatives
Found: 2026-09-28.  Status: 30+-digit spectrally-accurate 3D BZ quadrature; PSLQ over {1, G, G', G''}

THE QUESTION (plain English)
  The 3D analogue of Formula 7: in a diamond-structure crystal with two different atoms (zinc-blende
  type, simplest tight-binding model) the Berry curvature is a vector field in 3D momentum space that
  averages to zero. How large is it on average (mean square), exactly, as a function of the gap?

MODEL / SET-UP
  As in Formula 4(c): diamond lattice, cubic cell side a = 1, H = [[Delta, f],[f*, -Delta]],
  f = sum_{j=1..4} e^{ik.delta_j}, delta_j = (+-1,+-1,+-1)/4 with an even number of minus signs,
  physical orbital positions, lower band.  Omega_ij = Delta Im(d_i f* d_j f)/(2 (|f|^2+Delta^2)^{3/2}),
  |Omega|^2 = Omega_xy^2 + Omega_yz^2 + Omega_zx^2, averaged over the BZ.
  s = Delta^2;  G(s) = <1/(eps + 4 + s)>, eps = |f|^2 - 4 = 4(c1c2+c2c3+c3c1), c_i = cos(k_i/2);

     G(s) = (2 sqrt(4+s) - sqrt(16+s)) (2K(k)/pi)^2 / Delta^3,
     k^2 = 1/2 - [ (8+s) sqrt(16+s) - 16 sqrt(4+s) ] / (2 Delta^3);   derivatives below are d/ds.

THE FORMULA
     <|Omega|^2> = [ (s+8) G + (5s^2 + 76s + 128) G' + 2 s (s+4)(s+16) G'' ] / 1024.
  Values: s = 4 (Delta = 2): 0.00061796260033398792810807558533...
          s = 8 (the magic mass of Formula 4): 0.000215053551224648942995384265452...
          s = 2: 0.0015948106516860378983...;  s = 9: 0.000177802179088358877213937101921...

  ODE, so G, G', G'' are independent and the relation is unique.) Pattern parallels Formula 7
  (honeycomb: -(s+9)/384 [G + (s+3) G'], one derivative fewer). An analytic derivation is still open.

FORMULA 9 - How much of a quantum walker stays trapped forever on the anisotropic staggered Lieb
            lattice: exact flat-band weight of an edge site (elliptic K and Pi)
Found: 2026-09-28.  Status: PSLQ (50 digits) at 24 rational (k_x,k_y) pairs with the analytically
derived modulus and characteristic; coefficients read off exactly; formula checked at irrational
parameters (40 digits) and by exact diagonalisation of finite tori (flat weight exact to 1e-14 at
L = 18; time-averaged return probability -> w^2 as 1/L^2).  Novelty: NOT FOUND in literature searched.
==========================================================================================

THE QUESTION (plain English)
  Put a quantum particle on one of the "edge" atoms of the Lieb lattice and let it evolve freely
  (continuous-time quantum walk). Part of it spreads away, but part is caught in the flat band,
  whose states are standing waves that never move: it stays near the start forever. With equal
  bond staggering in x and y, symmetry gives exactly half. What fraction is trapped when the x- and
  y-bonds alternate differently?

MODEL / SET-UP
  Lieb lattice as in Formula 1 (hoppings 1 +- delta_x on the x-bonds, 1 +- delta_y on the y-bonds).
  Start on an x-edge site B. The trapped fraction is the flat-band weight
     w_B = <|g|^2/(|f|^2+|g|^2)>_BZ,
  the long-time average of the return probability is w_B^2 (the dispersive part decays), and the
  probability of finding the walker on the y-edge sites of the trapping modes is 1 - w_B.
  k_x = (1-dx^2)/(1+dx^2), k_y = (1-dy^2)/(1+dy^2). Elliptic integrals with PARAMETER m:
  K(m), Pi(n,m) (mpmath ellipk(m), ellippi(n,m)).

THE FORMULA
     w_B = 1 + (1/pi) [ (1-k_y) sqrt((1+k_x)/(1+k_y)) K(m)  -  2 (1 - k_x k_y)/sqrt((1+k_x)(1+k_y)) Pi(n,m) ],
     m = k_x k_y,   n = k_x (1+k_y)/(1+k_x).
  Isotropic check: k_x = k_y = k gives n = k, m = k^2, and with the classical identity
  Pi(k, k^2) = pi/(4(1-k)) + K/2 the formula reduces to exactly 1/2.
  Values: (dx,dy) = (0.37,0.81): w_B = 0.620665592107644625980176 (return prob. w^2 = 0.385225777226333095);
          (0.1,0.9): 0.6848550595480753333513507;  (0.05,0.3): 0.5482826408182812728002624;
          (1/2,1/3): 0.4525909186428942600282689.

DERIVATION (sketch)
  <b/S> = 1 - <a/S> with S = a + b; the ky-average is exact: <a/S>_y = a/sqrt((a+2p_y)^2 - 4q_y^2),
  a = 2p_x + 2q_x cos kx. The square root vanishes at cos kx = -(2+k_x-k_x k_y)/(k_x(1+k_y)) and
  -(2+k_x)/k_x, so with the branch points {1,-1} the cross-ratio gives m = k_x k_y and the pole gives the
  characteristic n = 2/(1-c_1) = k_x(1+k_y)/(1+k_x). PSLQ with basis {1, sK, sPi} (s = sqrt((1+k_y)/(1+k_x)))
  at 24 pairs gave coefficients A = (1+k_x)(1-k_y)/(1+k_y) and B = -2(1-k_x k_y)/(1+k_y), e.g.
  (1/3,5/6): A = 4/33, B = -26/33.

VERIFICATION
  1. Formula vs 40-digit quadrature: (0.37,0.81): -1e-41; (0.1,0.9): 0; (0.6,0.6): 0.5 exactly (diff -1e-41);
     (0.05,0.3): 1e-41. Symmetry w_B(dx,dy) + w_B(dy,dx) = 1 holds (0.45259.. + 0.54741.. = 1).
  2. Independent exact diagonalisation (trap_ed.py) of L x L tori at (0.37,0.81):
       flat-band weight on B: L=12: 0.6206655926461, L=18: 0.6206655921077, L=24: 0.6206655921076 (formula
       0.62066559210764);  long-time-average return probability: 0.38715, 0.38613, 0.38575 -> w^2 = 0.38523
       (excess 0.0019, 0.0009, 0.0005 ~ 1/L^2, the finite-size dispersive contribution).

  Searched: continuous-time quantum walk Lieb lattice flat band trapping probability localisation, exact,

FORMULA 10 - Strained / unequal-bond gapped honeycomb and diamond: the quantum metric for ARBITRARY
             bond strengths, and a universal rational value at the magic mass
             Delta*^2 = (z/(z-2)) (t_1^2 + ... + t_z^2)
Found: 2026-09-28.  Status: DERIVED (Formula 3/4 argument extended), verified by 40-50-digit spectral
BZ quadrature (honeycomb: magic value to 5e-53, general formula to 5e-41) and independent finite-
difference codes for honeycomb (3 cases, ~1e-11) and diamond with four unequal bonds (~5e-13).
Novelty: NOT FOUND in literature searched (extends Formulas 3-4).
==========================================================================================

THE QUESTION (plain English)
  Real graphene is often strained, so its three bonds have different strengths t1, t2, t3; the same
  holds for distorted diamond-type crystals. Does the exact result of Formulas 3-4 survive? Yes - and
  the special "magic" gap where the minimal Wannier spread becomes a simple fraction survives too:
  its location moves with the bond strengths, but the value at it does not change at all.

MODEL / SET-UP
  Bipartite simplex lattice as in Formula 4 (honeycomb z = 3, diamond z = 4), ideal geometry and
  physical orbital positions, but bond j has hopping t_j:  f = sum_j t_j e^{ik.delta_j}.
  H = [[Delta, f],[f*, -Delta]], lower band; T = sum_j t_j^2;  G = <1/(|f|^2 + Delta^2)>_BZ.

THE FORMULA
  Honeycomb (Bravais constant 1):   M = 1/48 + (3T - Delta^2) G / 48.
  General simplex lattice:          M = b^2(z-2)/(8(z-1)) + b^2 [ T/4 - (T+Delta^2)(z-2)/(8(z-1)) ] G.
  MAGIC MASS: at Delta^2 = (z/(z-2)) T the G-term vanishes and
      M = b^2 (z-2)/(8(z-1))   for ALL bond strengths
      (honeycomb: Delta^2 = 3(t1^2+t2^2+t3^2), M = 1/48;  diamond: Delta^2 = 2(t1^2+..+t4^2), M = 1/64).
  G for the honeycomb in closed form: the k2-average is exact,
      G = (1/pi) Int_0^pi dk1 / sqrt(A^2 - R^2),  A = T + Delta^2 + 2 t1 t2 cos k1,
      R^2 = 4 t3^2 (t1^2 + t2^2 + 2 t1 t2 cos k1),
  a complete elliptic integral of the first kind (quadratic under the root; reduce as in Formula 3).
  Example: (t1,t2,t3) = (1.3,0.8,1), Delta = 1: M = 0.0849374955787577668...

DERIVATION (sketch)
  The two lattice identities used in Formula 3 do not need equal bonds:
   |grad f|^2 = b^2 T + c (|f|^2 - T),  c = -b^2/(z-1)  (only the simplex geometry is used), and
   eps = |f|^2 - T is a sum of cosines of the equal-length difference vectors, so Laplacian(eps) = -L^2 eps.
  The divergence-theorem step then goes through unchanged, with w = T + Delta^2.

VERIFICATION
  1. Honeycomb, 50-digit periodic trapezoid (strained_hp.py), t = (1.3, 0.8, 1), Delta^2 = 3T:
     M - 1/48 = -4e-53 (N = 80), -4e-52 (N = 110).
  2. Honeycomb general formula vs 40-digit quadrature: t = (1.3,0.8,1), Delta = 1: 1.7e-41;
     t = (0.5,1,1.7), Delta^2 = 0.49: 5.2e-41.
  3. Independent finite differences (strained.py, 150^2 mesh):
     (1.3,0.8,1), Delta = 1: 0.08493749557569 (formula 0.08493749557876);
     (0.5,1,1.7), Delta = 0.7: 0.11330410221849 (formula 0.11330410222762);
     (1.3,0.8,1), magic Delta: 0.02083333333295 (1/48).
  4. Diamond with t = (1.2, 0.7, 1.0, 0.9) at Delta^2 = 2T (check_aniso_diamond.py, 48^3 mesh):
     0.01562499999948 (1/64).

NOVELTY

  was not seen. Status: not found in literature searched.
